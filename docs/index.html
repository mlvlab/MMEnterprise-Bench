Overview

Abstract

Enterprise agents answer questions over workplace records that evolve through team collaboration. As information is shared, corrected, and revised across documents, chats, and figures, multiple versions of the same information accumulate in the workspace. Reliable question answering therefore requires agents to reason over sufficient evidence, resolve cross-source and temporal conflicts, and recognize when the available evidence is insufficient to establish an answer. To systematically evaluate these capabilities, we introduce MMEnterprise-Bench, a benchmark comprising 1,613 questions grounded in evolving multimodal records from simulated collaboration projects, covering three categories: Evidence Reasoning, Conflict Resolution, and Unanswerability. We evaluate 10 proprietary and open-source multimodal LLMs and find that even the strongest models struggle when evidence contains cross-source or temporal conflicts or spans multiple images. Moreover, increasing the retrieval budget or employing a larger retriever improves evidence recall but does not consistently translate into higher answer accuracy. These findings highlight the persistent challenges of reasoning over evolving workplace records and establish MMEnterprise-Bench as a testbed for developing more reliable enterprise agents.

ER

Evidence Reasoning

Answer from sufficient, non-conflicting evidence spread across chats, emails, meeting notes, trackers, reports, and slides: extraction, attribution, computation, comparison, and interpretation.

CR

Conflict Resolution

Earlier records are retained rather than overwritten, so superseded values stay in the corpus. Agents must pick the value that applies across sources, or recover the state at a specific point in time.

UNS

Unanswerability

Questions built by perturbing one entity or condition of an answerable question, so that no record in the project supports an answer. The agent has to say so instead of guessing.

Benchmark construction

How the benchmark is built

1

Workspace construction

Text, tables, and figures are extracted from 30 public papers and reports. For each project, a team with roles, channels, access relationships, and deadlines is specified, and an LLM plans collaborative events (result sharing, error detection, corrections, document revisions) in chronological order. Events are realized as messages, emails, meeting notes, trackers, reports, and slides; earlier versions are kept.

2

QA generation

Rule-based generation covers questions derivable from structured project information; an LLM with task-specific instructions generates the rest, jointly producing the question, reference answer, step-by-step solution, and supporting records. Unanswerable questions perturb one entity or condition of an answerable question so that no project record supports an answer.

3

Quality control & human validation

Independent LLM-based checks verify records against the source and planned events, and verify that the cited evidence derives the reference answer, flagging ambiguity, wrong answers, and leakage. In a blind audit of 100 annotations by three annotators, 71% were rejected by no one and 29% by at least one; none of the 50 LLM-accepted items was rejected by any annotator, and all 29 human-rejected items had also been rejected by the LLM.

Evaluation protocol. For each question, a Qwen3-VL-Embedding retriever (2B by default; 8B also released) ranks the 7,140 workspace records; the top-k records are presented to the model ordered by timestamp and ID, each as its text followed by its image when it has one. An LLM judge (GPT-5.6 Luna) compares the response with the reference answer, and accuracy is reported over all 1,613 questions. Both retriever rankings and the judge prompt are released, so results are reproducible without re-running retrieval.

Analysis

Where enterprise agents fail

  • Conflicts are the hard partAcross models, cross-source accuracy ranges from 11.8% to 36.3% and temporal accuracy from 13.2% to 33.5% at k = 10, far below extraction from a single record.
  • Multiple images hurt mostGPT-5.6 Sol drops from 83.3% on single-text-record questions to 52.9% with one image and 27.5% with two or more images.
  • Recall is not accuracyRaising k from 50 to 100 lifts recall from 57.0% to 64.8%, yet Qwen3.5-2B falls from 25.0% to 22.2%; the 8B retriever adds recall but not consistently accuracy.
